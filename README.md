## 2. Project Background

AIDS Programs South Saskatchewan (APSS) is a Regina-based non-profit organization providing harm reduction, needle exchange, testing, education, outreach and support related to HIV/AIDS and hepatitis C.

APSS's main website already serves people who use its programs and services. However, APSS has partnered with the University of Regina on two research projects connected to reportneedles.ca, its tool for mapping reported needle hot spots, and the resulting research has no dedicated home online. Our team is designing a separate research website that sits alongside the main APSS site. It will present current and completed projects, journal publications, research posters and a research blog, and it will let visitors move easily between the research site, the main APSS website and reportneedles.ca.

## 3. Business Need / Opportunity

Because APSS research has no dedicated home, it is hard for visitors to find and is not presented in one place. A dedicated research website gives APSS a clear, trustworthy and accessible place to:

- showcase its research projects and their status
- share publications and posters with researchers, partners and funders
- keep the community informed through research updates
- connect visitors to the main APSS website and reportneedles.ca

The site should feel authentic, welcoming and easy to use.

## 4. Northstar and Carryover Customers

**Project goal:** Create a welcoming and accessible digital entry point that helps people quickly find APSS research, projects and updates.

**Northstar customer:** APSS, represented by Erin Hidlebaugh (Program Coordinator), the organization whose research the site presents.

**Carryover customers:** Existing APSS audiences with an interest in its research, including University of Regina research collaborators, community partners, funders and community members. The site should give them clear access to projects, publications, posters and updates, with easy navigation between the research site, the main APSS website and reportneedles.ca.

## 5. Assumptions

- The site will be built on WordPress.
- The research site is separate from the main APSS website, which will not be redesigned.
- APSS will provide the content: project descriptions, publications, posters and blog posts.
- APSS staff, who may not be technical, will update the site after handoff.
- Visitors will have different levels of knowledge about APSS and its research.
- Clear and inclusive language is essential.

## 6. Constraints

- **Platform:** Everything must work within WordPress themes and plugins, so the site can be maintained without custom code.
- **Maintainability:** Non-technical staff must be able to add projects, publications and posts on their own.
- **Sensitive subject matter:** Content about HIV, hepatitis C and drug use must avoid stigma and must never identify people who use APSS services.
- **Consent and authenticity:** Images must be authentic and used with consent.
- **Publication rights:** Some journal articles may be behind paywalls or under copyright, so the site may need to link to them rather than host the files.
- **Accessibility:** The design must meet accessibility standards (for example, WCAG 2.1 AA) for a diverse audience.
- **Consistency across sites:** The research site must feel connected to the main APSS website and reportneedles.ca.
- **Evolving requirements:** The client's requirements are still developing, so the design must adapt after each check-in.
